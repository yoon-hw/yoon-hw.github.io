I am a Ph.D. student in Urban and Regional Science at Texas A&M University. I focus on how disasters reshape neighborhoods and what those changes mean for public health. My work combines computer vision, street-level imagery, mobility data, and geospatial data science to measure built-environment conditions and human activities.

Research

Neighborhood Physical Disorder after Hurricanes

A two-step deep learning pipeline detects physical disorder from Google Street View imagery, tracking how neighborhood conditions in Houston shifted before and after Hurricane Harvey. This work also examines how those changes shape physical activity in the affected neighborhoods.

Neighborhood Environments and Health

Where people live shapes how well they live. One line of this work links neighborhood environmental conditions to cancer-related hospital utilization across Texas, combining census tract and ZIP code level data. Another, a meta-analysis of 50 studies, shows that human movement rather than static density drove COVID-19 transmission, pointing to gatherings and mobility as the mechanism behind infectious disease spread in cities.

Human Activity from Mobility Data

Smartphone mobility and POI data capture where people actually go, at a scale surveys cannot reach. Applied to Austin, these traces measure who reaches urban parks, how highways cut off nearby green space, and what recreational value those spaces deliver.
